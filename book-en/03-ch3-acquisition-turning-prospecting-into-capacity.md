# Chapter 3 Acquisition: AI Turns Prospecting into Capacity

## 3.1 Artisan: The Company That Printed "Stop Hiring Humans" on a Billboard

In 2025, a series of billboards appeared on the streets of San Francisco — white background, black text, a single line: "Stop Hiring Humans." They were signed by a startup called Artisan, whose product is Ava, an AI sales agent that automatically finds prospects, researches leads, writes personalized emails, follows up, and books meetings — reportedly at one-fifth the cost of a human BDR (business development representative), with more than 300 million verified B2B contacts built in<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-15">[3-15]</a></sup>[official claims].

The company later raised a $25 million Series A. Business Insider exclusively reported on its fundraising materials — which promised not "sales assistance" but "replacing human sales with AI"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-14">[3-14]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

The other side of the story is equally telling. In late 2025, Artisan's AI SDR, Ava, was banned from LinkedIn — because its automated way of contacting prospects violated the platform's rules. CEO Jaspar Carmichael-Jack confirmed the ban to TechCrunch; the account was restored two weeks later<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-44">[3-44]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Around the same time, the founder publicly admitted that early versions of the product had hallucination problems and that the company had gone through customer churn<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#12-16">[12-16]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

A company built on "stop hiring humans" — banned by a platform, disciplined by customer churn — and then it came back. That complexity is an accurate portrait of AI-driven acquisition as a field: **the efficiency is real, and so is the backlash.**

## 3.2 AI SDR: From Tool to Digital Employee

First, consider the size of the market. The AI SDR (sales development representative) category was worth roughly $4.27 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $18.19 billion by 2032 (a 23% compound annual growth rate); over the past two years, venture capital has poured more than $1 billion into AI SDR startups<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-07">[3-07]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

Product forms have already evolved through two generations:

**The first generation is "assistive tools":** they help human SDRs write emails, find leads, and prioritize — the human remains the lead actor.

**The second generation is "digital employees":** 11x's product line is the most representative — Alice (email + LinkedIn outreach), Julian (phone agent), and other "digital employees" hold their own phone numbers and, 24/7 with no human supervision, make calls, answer calls, screen leads, and book meetings, automatically writing call data back to the CRM<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-38">[3-38]</a></sup>[official claims]. Artisan's Ava covers the full flow of "lead research → personalized email → follow-up sequences"[official claims]. AiSDR's own ROI calculation is more direct: an AI SDR subscription costs about 85% less than hiring an in-house SDR, with similar output<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-12">[3-12]</a></sup>[official claims].

Both capital and vendors are telling a story of "replacement." But independent data gives a more complicated answer.

## 3.3 AI vs. Human SDRs: What the Head-to-Head Data Says

Salesmotion's aggregation of adversarial head-to-head tests offers a rare direct comparison[third-party verified]:

- **Revenue**: human SDRs generated 2.6x the revenue of AI SDRs<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-06">[3-06]</a></sup> ($147,000 vs. $56,000);
- **Meeting show-up rate**: humans 71%, AI 52%;
- **Cost**: a human SDR's fully loaded cost is $100,000–150,000 per year; an AI SDR license costs roughly $30,000 per year.

AI delivers volume hundreds of times greater than humans; humans deliver quality two to three times higher than AI. When the full math is done, the conclusion is not "who replaces whom" but a division of labor: **AI owns breadth and first-touch outreach; humans own high-value conversion and complex relationships.**

This conclusion is corroborated at a grander level. Sequoia Capital's investment thesis — "services as the new software" — argues: if you sell tools (software), you are racing against the models; if you sell the work itself (service outcomes), every model upgrade makes your service faster and cheaper<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-45">[3-45]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Harvard Business Review defines agentic AI as a paradigm-level variable in sales: it makes a "perfect replica of the top salesperson" possible — autonomous agents unconstrained by time or geography, working side by side with human sellers<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-42">[3-42]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

Note HBR's wording: **working side by side**, not replacing. The mainstream of 2026 is not "AI replaces salespeople" but a hybrid model of "AI sales + human sales" — Chapter 8 will present more detailed ROI data from controlled experiments on this model.

Chinese exporters offer a striking counterpoint. Jintai Technology (Chinese: 今泰科技), a Guangzhou company that applies superhard film coatings to metal surfaces, once sent 80,000 cold emails to overseas buyers in a single burst, almost all of which sank without a response. After adopting a sales agent from Tungee (Chinese: 探迹科技), salespeople only enter product information and target markets, and the agent finds buyers worldwide, drafts the outreach emails, and follows up across time zones; within three months its number of inbound inquiries equaled the total acquired over the previous three years, email reply rates rose at least tenfold, and it broke into the Western tableware industry that had long resisted its approach<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-80">[3-80]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Same channel, same email — output differed by three orders of magnitude. The difference was not volume but the data layer: the quality of buyer profiling and filtering determines the efficiency of every step that follows.

## 3.4 Content Acquisition: AI Turns "Writing" into "Manufacturing"

If AI SDR is proactive outreach, content acquisition is building a nest to attract the phoenix. The killer weapon of AI content acquisition is **programmatic SEO**: use AI to mass-produce pages targeting long-tail keywords, so search engines deliver a steady stream of precise traffic.

Three frequently cited cases:

**Zapier**: monthly visits grew from roughly 1.19 million in November 2020 to roughly 4.8 million in November 2023, an increase of more than 300% in three years. Its core assets are more than 6 million backlinks and tens of thousands of automation tutorial pages on "how to connect A to B"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-22">[3-22]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**Jasper**: in 2021, this AI writing company had 14 employees in total — and just one in marketing. It outsourced its "content growth engine" to a specialist agency, and the result was 810% growth in organic blog traffic, 400x growth in blog-driven signups, and $4 million in ARR<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-24">[3-24]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**An AI image-generation SaaS**: previously 67 signups a month and 13 ranking keywords. After a programmatic SEO overhaul, monthly signups climbed from 67 to more than 2,100 within 10 months, and organic traffic grew 3,035%<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-28">[3-28]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

By 2026, the content acquisition battlefield has expanded from "search engines" to "AI search." GEO (generative engine optimization) has become a new term: optimize your brand content so it appears in the output of ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity — the goal is to become the source an AI cites when it answers a user's question<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-19">[3-19]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Glasp pushes the practice further: this tool with 3.5 million users grew traffic from ChatGPT from 500 sessions a day to 19,000 — not by guessing how AI thinks, but by reading its own server logs to see directly which pages ChatGPT visited and cited<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-51">[3-51]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. In the AI era, content acquisition is shifting from "guessing the algorithm" to "observing the AI."

## 3.5 ABM × AI: Treating Every Key Account as Its Own Market

The logic of ABM (account-based marketing) is: don't cast a wide net — lock onto a small number of high-value target accounts and run each one like its own "market."

AI makes ABM scalable for the first time. Demandbase divides AI's capabilities into three layers: NLP that reads unstructured data from web pages, search, and social media for intent monitoring; generative AI that automatically produces personalized content for each account; and predictive models that rank accounts<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-10">[3-10]</a></sup>[official claims]. 6sense's platform uses predictive analytics and intent data to help companies discover and rank target accounts that are "entering their buying window"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-05">[3-05]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

Key data supports this direction: roughly 70% of the B2B buying journey is completed anonymously before buyers ever contact a vendor<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-34">[3-34]</a></sup>[official claims] — they research, compare, and read reviews online first, and by the time a salesperson reaches out, half the decision is already made. The value of intent data is turning that "invisible shopping" into observable signals: whoever is intensively researching a category is a buyer in the market at this moment.

Chinese practice is keeping pace. SalesDriver's white paper shows that about 26.48% of companies have rolled out ABM, up 149% from the prior year<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-30">[3-30]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. SANY (Chinese: 三一重工) is a sample from large-scale B2B manufacturing: in 2022, its overseas revenue grew 47% and accounted for 45% of total revenue, while revenue from developed markets in Europe and the Americas reached roughly RMB 15.8 billion, up 86% year over year<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-40">[3-40]</a></sup>[third-party verified] — its playbook is not internet-style traffic operations but running target customers as "key accounts," supported by coordinated channel and content work (strictly speaking, not textbook ABM, but aligned in direction).

## 3.6 Private Domain: An AI Battleground Unique to the Chinese Market

If ABM is the Western B2B playbook, the private domain (Chinese: 私域) is China's own customer-operations battleground — adding customers to WeChat or WeCom (Chinese: 企业微信) and repeatedly reaching out to, nurturing, and converting them there.

The biggest pain point of the private domain is "added, then silent": large numbers of new contacts stop interacting within two weeks of being added. NetEase Zhiqi's (Chinese: 网易智企) diagnosis is sharp — the causes fall into two categories: silent contacts (never responded) and dormant old customers (bought before but inactive recently) — while blanket group broadcasts fail because of message folding, do-not-disturb settings, and mismatched content<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-41">[3-41]</a></sup>[official claims]. AI's solution is "segment people first, then match content": a three-layer filter (basic attributes, behavioral traces, relationship stage) divides contacts into tiers, and each tier is paired with a different AI outreach strategy<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-47">[3-47]</a></sup>[official claims].

Effect data comes from Shuyun (Chinese: 数云): during the 618 shopping festival, a well-known personal care brand's private-domain traffic pool surged beyond its reception capacity; after AI digital employees took over, the new-customer reception rate rose 140%, the conversion rate rose 29.2%, and labor costs fell 61.3%<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-43">[3-43]</a></sup>[official claims]. Tencent Qidian (Chinese: 腾讯企点) serves more than 1 million enterprises and connects 350 million users, embedding large models into its marketing cloud and intelligent customer service<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-46">[3-46]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

## 3.7 Paid Media: AI Turns Budgets into Algorithms

Traditional paid media relies on manual bid adjustment and trial and error. AI-powered ad products turn this into an algorithmic problem: with Meta's Advantage+ campaigns, CPA (cost per acquisition) fell 12–17%, reaching 32% in some cases<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-27">[3-27]</a></sup>[third-party verified]; one Google Performance Max campaign automatically runs across Search, YouTube, Display, and Gmail<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-20">[3-20]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

At the budget-allocation level, the 2026 consensus is that MTA (multi-touch attribution) and MMM (marketing mix modeling) must run in parallel: MTA answers the tactical question — "which channel converted my customers" — while MMM answers the strategic one — "how the overall budget should be split"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-26">[3-26]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Causal-AI versions of MMM go further: after causaLens helped a large consumer app company rebuild its attribution model, its annual marketing budget fell 5% and ROI reached 15x<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-36">[3-36]</a></sup>[official claims].

## 3.8 The Backlash: AI Outbound Is Killing Cold Email

At this point in Chapter 3, we have to pause to cover the flip side — because the biggest risk in AI-driven acquisition is not that AI is too weak, but that AI is too easily abused, and abuse can destroy the entire channel.

The data is alarming. A cohort study of 14 B2B SaaS sales organizations found that reply rates to AI SDR outreach decayed by more than 60% within 18 months — from 11.2% at launch down to 4.4% by month 18 — because recipients gradually learned to recognize the structure of AI templates<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-32">[3-32]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. The cold email ecosystem as a whole is worse off: 95% of cold emails now get zero replies at all; when personalization is traded for speed and volume, reply rates run 13x lower than normal<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-39">[3-39]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Cold call reply rates fell from 8.5% in 2018 to 4.2% in 2022[official claims].

The Pipeline Group's warning is harsher: running outbound sales at scale with AI SDRs is dragging companies into a state that is "illegal, unethical, and brand-destroying" — AI SDRs often use scraped data obtained without consent, crossing compliance red lines such as GDPR and CCPA<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-09">[3-09]</a></sup>[official claims].

Hence a paradox that emerged in 2026: **AI has driven the cost of proactive outreach toward zero, and it has also driven the value of proactive outreach toward zero.** When everyone can send ten thousand emails with AI, recipients learn to ignore all emails. The first users consumed the channel's dividend; the imitators who followed only ate the backlash.

That explains why the acquisition consensus in 2026 is turning toward "relationship-driven": rather than casting a wide net with AI, use AI to precisely identify "who is in the market right now" (intent data), then reach them in a high-quality, personalized way. The second half of AI-driven acquisition is a contest of restraint, not intensity.

The mechanism of that backlash went one level deeper in 2026: detection no longer depends on a recipient's impression but on detectable watermarks and platform rules. Anthropic's Claude now embeds invisible watermarks in all generated text (a global change driven by European Union regulatory requirements), which means AI-drafted sales emails can be identified as machine-generated; at the same time, fully AI-generated cold emails receive fewer replies and are flagged as spam at a higher rate<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-78">[3-78]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Anti-AI outreach has moved from "a person can tell something is off" to "a system can measure it."

One practitioner's account puts a number on the slope: he spent more than 400 hours building his own AI SDR for cold-email prospecting and ultimately lifted reply rates only from 1% to 2%, while spending the same effort on phone outreach produced five times more pipeline, at a cost far below those 400 hours. The industry's average email reply rate fell from 8.5% in 2019 to 3.4% in 2026 — AI accelerated that decline because it lets everyone, including your competitors, pour more volume into the same inbox, while buyers, Gmail, and Outlook all learned to filter. The constraint on the phone side is a hard constraint: connect rates have long hovered at 3–5%, and statistics across billions of dials show that only about 20% of people in any market will answer a cold call, while the other 80% never will regardless of how, from what number, or at what hour you call — the AI SDR inherited this constraint rather than solving it<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-79">[3-79]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

## 3.9 The AI-ization of Advertising and the Truthfulness of Traffic

Section 3.7 described how AI turns ad budgets into algorithms. Since then, two new shifts have appeared in this channel: the entry points are migrating, and the credibility of measurement is weakening.

**Migrating entry points.** In September 2026, Amazon and OpenAI struck a partnership: advertisers can extend campaigns through Amazon's DSP (demand-side platform) into ChatGPT's conversational ad slots, which surface as text and image units beneath the answers with clearly marked "sponsored" labels; the pilot is limited to the U.S. at launch, and Delta Vacations is among the first brands. ChatGPT Ads has been live for a little over half a year and has reached a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate, with tens of thousands of advertisers using it<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-52">[3-52]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. When "conversation" itself becomes ad inventory, the AI assistant stops being a "substitute for search" and becomes "a new medium" — acquisition entry points are no longer just search engines and social platforms, but also the conversation between a brand and an AI assistant.

**Conversion quality.** The new entry point's traffic quality is documented: Semrush's search research found that users arriving via AI-generated answers convert 4.4 times better than those arriving from traditional search results<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-53">[3-53]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. The intent is clearer — the user has already walked to the last step of the decision, and the AI merely walks them to the door.

**But the foundation of measurement is loosening.** An indie developer ran a Google app-install campaign for his puzzle app and was billed for 56 installs in two weeks; going back through the raw panel data, 33 of them showed the same "bot pattern" — an old app version the store had stopped serving, a single open per device, zero seconds on any screen, and 28 phone models across 19 states<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-54">[3-54]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. The loop is the ironic part: Google optimizes for whatever goal the advertiser sets, and "a view followed by an install" counts as a conversion, so the more the farm "installed" the app, the more budget the algorithm sent the farm's way, and the spend was systematically fed to bots. His fix was to change the campaign goal from "opened the app" to "won a puzzle" — making faking more expensive than genuinely playing.

For the acquisition stage of AI-powered customer lifecycle management, the lesson is valuable: when a channel's raw metric can be forged by machines, **the objective function must land on deep behaviors that only real customers produce**. Entry points can go AI-native; measurement cannot be lazy.

## 3.10 Entry Points: In the Chinese Market, the Search Box Is Turning into a Dialogue Box

The previous sections described the migration of entry points in the English-speaking world. The Chinese path is different — no single search company dominates it, and the redistribution of entry points is happening simultaneously across three lines: super apps, standalone AI apps, and the browser layer.

**WeChat: a first-class entry point.** WeChat is testing an upgrade that promotes AI search from a second-level feature to a first-class entry point: tapping the search box at the top of the app invokes AI search, with an optional "deep thinking" mode and support for uploading images or files, plus a separate "Ask Yuanbao (Chinese: 元宝)" entry that routes users to Tencent's native AI app. Deep-thinking queries default to the DeepSeek R1 model, image search to the Hunyuan T1 model. Tencent's official line is that search is continuously improving its AI capabilities and the feature remains in gray-scale testing<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-55">[3-55]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. The three Chinese giants are effectively running the same play — retrofit the super app with AI while funneling users toward a standalone AI app: Tencent pairs WeChat with Yuanbao, ByteDance pairs Douyin (Chinese: 抖音) with Doubao (Chinese: 豆包), and Baidu pairs its mobile app with Wenxiaoyan.

**Browsers: making AI the system layer.** In November 2025, Qwen (Chinese: 千问) was deeply integrated with a new version of the Quark (Chinese: 夸克) AI browser; Qwen no longer exists as a plug-in but is embedded as the interface's foundation, callable directly from the home-page search box and the sidebar and pinnable as a desktop companion. Quark's AI browser has reached 110 million installs on PCs, and the Qwen app passed 10 million downloads within a week of its public beta<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-56">[3-56]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. IDC projects that by 2028 annual shipments of AI-capable PCs in China will be 60 times the 2024 figure. What matters here is not a "smarter search box" but screen reading, text selection, and screenshot capture — AI entering the price-comparison and purchase decisions a user is making right now.

**Why entry points get reassigned.** In March 2025 Alibaba promoted Quark to an "AI super box" and designated it the company's flagship AI application; the team later concluded that a conversational assistant was the better form factor, and resources and narrative shifted to Qwen. The arithmetic behind that decision is user scale: QuestMobile data for September 2025 put Doubao at 172 million monthly active users, DeepSeek at 145 million, and Tencent Yuanbao at 32.86 million, while Tongyi ranked only tenth at 3.06 million<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-57">[3-57]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. Content, social, and search are held by ByteDance, Tencent, and Baidu respectively; what Alibaba lacked was exactly a traffic entry point — hence the conclusion that it "must have an AI-native consumer super entry point."

**In-platform search is becoming a primary battlefield too.** Douyin's e-commerce arm states officially that 80% of its monthly active users initiate searches and search an average of 100 times per month; daily e-commerce search users reach 140 million with close to 500 million daily search page views, and the conversion efficiency of searched products is 90% higher than in other scenarios<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-58">[3-58]</a></sup> [official claims]. A frontline operator describes an even more thorough algorithmic shift: 47% of Douyin users proactively initiate decision-oriented searches, proactive search page views grew 36% year over year and search GMV grew 55%, the weight of short-video algorithm signals in search ranking jumped from 35% to 61%, and the useful life of a keyword strategy compressed from three to six months down to two to four weeks<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-59">[3-59]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. Content format directly determines recall: a smart-glasses category that rewrote technical specs as scenario-based expressions lifted search conversion by 91%, and an outdoor brand that broke product descriptions into five-second scene clips quadrupled click-through.

For acquisition, the first question has therefore changed: not "where do my keywords rank" but "in which interface will my customer ask their question." A search box, a dialogue box, and a browser's screen-reading layer are three different sets of content and data requirements.

Zoom out one more step and a more easily overlooked fact appears: in China, one of the largest entry points for "AI search" is not a standalone app but the search-box upgrade inside a super app. QuestMobile data shows WeChat's AI search reached 160 million monthly active users in June 2025, more than native AI apps such as Doubao and Yuanbao, though still below Baidu and Douyin<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-81">[3-81]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Users need neither to install a new app nor to change their habits — the AI capability is placed inside an existing high-frequency action, which means that watching only the MAU rankings of standalone AI apps systematically underestimates the real reach of AI embedded in super apps.

## 3.11 AI Visibility: A Second Report Card Beyond Rankings

If customers ask their questions inside a dialogue box, then "ranking" is no longer a complete report card. In 2026 an entirely new set of metrics appeared: whether a brand is named in an AI answer, and who was cited when it was named.

**The volume is still small, but the direction is clear.** Conductor's 2026 AEO/GEO Benchmarks Report puts AI referral traffic at just over 1% of total website visits, growing by roughly one percentage point per month on average; its value lies not in traffic volume but in the signal that a brand made it into an AI answer — what the report calls a "parallel surface of visibility," where discovery now happens before a user reaches the website at all<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-60">[3-60]</a></sup> [third-party verified].

**Who gets cited?** Peec AI's analysis of 30 million sources found Reddit to be the most-cited domain across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, and AI Overviews, followed by YouTube, LinkedIn, Wikipedia, and Forbes; review platforms such as Yelp and G2 appear frequently in "best X" recommendation queries. The same research quantifies the filter: only 15% of pages ChatGPT retrieves appear in the final answer, and 44% of citations come from the first third of the body text<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-61">[3-61]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. 5W's citation source audit puts platform divergence even more starkly: only about 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity, so a single content strategy cannot span the AI surfaces; Wikipedia accounts for roughly 47.9% of ChatGPT's top-ten source share while Reddit accounts for about 46.7% of Perplexity's; YouTube correlates with AI visibility at 0.737, the strongest single predictor in the 2025–2026 studies; and LinkedIn moved from eleventh to fifth place in ChatGPT within three months<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-62">[3-62]</a></sup> [third-party verified].

**The technical barrier matters more than the content barrier.** After analyzing more than a million citations, OtterlyAI found that brands' own domains account for 52.5% of all citations, with the rest flowing to news sites (20.3%) and community forums (5.9%). The most actionable finding: 73% of websites block AI crawlers somewhere — in robots.txt, CDN rules, or JavaScript rendering<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-63">[3-63]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. In other words, many brands are eliminated at the crawling stage before citation ever becomes possible — fixing crawlability outranks writing more content.

**This is still a discipline in trial and error.** Two GEO experiments logged by Search Engine Land, covering 775 citation events in total, concluded that "who mentions you" may matter more than "what you write" in determining whether AI recommends you: the operational focus shifts from optimizing your own content to getting third-party sources — reviews, directories, forums, media — to mention you on the relevant topics. The author also admits an important caveat: a conclusion from the first experiment did not survive in the second, showing how easily a single GEO experiment mistakes platform noise for causal law and that findings require cross-platform, cross-cycle replication<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-64">[3-64]</a></sup> [third-party verified].

**A methodology has already taken shape in the Chinese market.** The core judgment of the ifenxi (Chinese: 爱分析) 2026 China GEO Market Research Report is that GEO is not a continuation of SEO — SEO optimizes search ranking while GEO optimizes AI cognition, and the chain changes from "search → impression → click → conversion" to "user intent → AI cognition → AI recommendation → user decision," with a ranking algorithm as one arbiter and a model's understanding and reasoning as the other. The same research points to brands' real dilemma: marketing teams want to use existing CDP data for GEO, but CDP data is largely accumulated by ad platforms and covers only existing customers, missing the new cognition that forms in AI scenarios<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-65">[3-65]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. That is why GEO deployment forces a data-layer rebuild rather than remaining a content outsourcing service.

## 3.12 Agentish: The Boundary of Autonomous Prospecting

The entry points changed and the metrics changed, but one thing did not: buyer intent does not become readable just because AI can watch for it around the clock.

**The first reason for skepticism is the signal itself.** Forrester lists seven reasons to be skeptical of agentic prospecting; the first and most fundamental is that autonomous prospecting assumes a quality of signal that does not exist. Web visits, content consumption, hiring signals, and keyword activity may indicate "interest" but rarely indicate "intent" reliably; intent is actually shaped by internal politics, budget timing, risk appetite, and hidden stakeholders — factors far beyond what any external signal stream can consistently capture<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-66">[3-66]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. This does not deny AI's value in outbound (filtering, research, timing prompts), but it does mean an agent's ability to execute should not be treated as evidence that a customer has a need.

**"Agentish" is a usable design standard.** The same firm offers a taxonomy: agentish means AI assists, suggests, and executes inside explicit workflows, guardrails, and approval thresholds, with humans still accountable for intent, outcomes, and risk; agentic means the system can set its own goals, change strategy, and execute end to end. The author's judgment is that most revenue technology platforms today are agentish, and that this is a deliberate design choice — in domains where revenue, compliance, and trust cannot be gambled on trial and error, each additional level of autonomy also complicates explainability and the chain of accountability<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-67">[3-67]</a></sup> [third-party verified]. Written into procurement and architecture standards, it becomes one sentence: keep human accountability and approval thresholds for forecasting, qualification, quoting, and customer promises; reserve autonomous execution for low-risk, reversible, measurable actions.

**Inside the boundary, scale can be enormous.** The sample Siemens and Salesforce announced at Dreamforce is "AI handles all inbound" rather than "AI handles some leads": Agentforce qualifies every inbound lead for Siemens' 18,000 sellers, service engineers can identify the correct spare part by serial number before the first site visit, and sales reps can only quote technically valid upgrade options<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-68">[3-68]</a></sup> [official claims]. The vendor calls this agent-to-agent AI, but the more useful takeaway is its view of where differentiation comes from: an industrial company's advantage is not replying faster but replying with engineering-correct content — making the product and configuration knowledge base the backend constraint on the agent is the precondition for this kind of scale.

## 3.13 "AI Visibility" Has Grown a Dashboard — and a Price Tag

The judgment in Section 3.11 was priced by the capital markets within a few months. Profound, an AI search visibility company barely 18 months old, raised a $96 million Series C at a $1 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed Venture Partners with Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins following on, bringing total funding to $155 million; its product traces how AI models describe and recommend brands across millions of real prompts, answering not only "did I show up" but "why did I show up" and "what are competitors doing differently"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-69">[3-69]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Whether a channel link genuinely exists is a question the capital markets often answer more directly than industry white papers.

**Measurement is being formalized.** A critical survey of GEO covering 2023–2026 formalizes the goal as "the presence, likelihood of citation, or influence of content in the answers produced by generative engines," treating citation probability and influence as two distinct optimization objectives rather than a single ranking<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-70">[3-70]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. The implication is clear: AI visibility is not mystical exposure but an engineering problem with an objective function.

**But the dashboard's readings are wildly unstable.** Semrush's weekly snapshots of 130,000 prompts over 13 weeks show that around mid-September 2025, ChatGPT's citations of Reddit plunged from about 60% of responses to about 10%, while Wikipedia fell from about 55% to under 20%; over the same window, Google AI Mode's and Perplexity's citations of both were essentially stable<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-71">[3-71]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Platform tastes differ just as sharply: AI Mode's top cited domains are almost entirely Google-owned or deeply partnered assets (LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit), while ChatGPT's winners that round were PRNewswire, Forbes, and Medium. The conclusion for spend is direct — **a single-point snapshot of visibility cannot serve as the basis for channel budget; trend monitoring is mandatory, along with accepting that "the same asset has entirely different value in different engines."**

**The fuel that actually gets cited is third-party evidence.** After analyzing more than 25 million citation links across ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini in 17 industries, Muck Rack found that earned media — press coverage, academic research, government and institutional sources — accounts for 84% of all AI citations, paid and sponsored content for only 0.3%, and news alone for 27% of cited sources; the share held stable between 82% and 89% across three versions<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-72">[3-72]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Platform differences are just as pronounced: ChatGPT cites in 96% of responses but averages only five citations, Gemini cites in 82% of responses averaging eight, and Claude is the pickiest — citing in only 55% of responses but averaging 13 citations when it does; their most-cited domains are Wikipedia, PubMed Central, and Reddit respectively. **One set of copy cannot serve all three; choosing the platform matters more than choosing the keyword.**

**Demand is shifting in step.** G2's survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers and decision-makers found that 51% now "start research from an AI chatbot more often," up from 29% eleven months earlier; 71% rely on AI chatbots for at least one stage of the research process; and 85% rate a vendor more highly when that vendor appears in AI answers<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-73">[3-73]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. G2 frames this as buyers' starting point moving from reference to inference — no longer asking the chatbot for directions, but letting it synthesize the shortlist directly, which means AI answers determine who makes the shortlist at all.

**And preferences form even earlier.** Forrester points out that most B2B buying journeys are not "selection" but "confirmation": more than two-thirds (68%) of buyers already have a preferred vendor when they begin procurement, so intent signals about "who is in market" alone are insufficient to win deals — the state of preference and the path by which it forms (who shapes it through which information sources) are the key variables<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-74">[3-74]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. That is why investment in AI visibility has to be front-loaded before buyers start buying — by the time the intent signal fires, the room to choose has already narrowed.

**Finally, AI entry points bring more than incremental traffic.** A randomized field experiment with 1,100 U.S. Chrome users found that those forced onto Google AI Mode sent 18.8 percentage points fewer searches to external websites than they did in normal use, running 0.92 fewer search sessions per day; by category, the share of users clicking through to news sites fell 12.5 percentage points, to Reddit 21.2 points, and to Wikipedia 9.9 points — and this diversion did not produce any gain in search satisfaction<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-75">[3-75]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. This is the closest thing to causal evidence available: **brands cannot shift budgets on the single vector of "AI traffic is growing" without also counting how much of their traditional search asset was cut away — on the same sheet.**

## 3.14 Two Falsifications of New Channels: Agent Stores and GEO Franchising

The most common failure mode of a new channel is not "it doesn't work" but "its effect cannot be verified, yet someone is already making money from it."

**The first falsification is that the channel itself does not hold up.** In July 2026, Doubao, Qwen, and Yuanbao almost simultaneously shut down their user-built agent marketplaces. The postmortems produced ugly numbers: Doubao's marketplace had accumulated roughly 8 million user-built agents and sat on about 200 million daily active users, but cost 130–240 million yuan per day against less than 1 million yuan of daily revenue; Yuanbao had about 9 million DAU after Tencent spent roughly 15 billion yuan on marketing, with extremely low retention. Overseas comparators are no brighter: of roughly 3 million GPTs created, only about 159,000 are public and active, median creator income is $0–100 per month, and the revenue-share formula remains unclear; Microsoft shut down Copilot GPTs less than three months after launch. Two causes stand out. First, user-built agents are essentially content generators, and the review burden shifts from reviewing one article at a time to reviewing all of its conversation paths, turning cost from O(N) into M×K^L — a one-time pre-launch review cannot, in principle, stop the risk. Second, creator revenue share is a cost line in the platform's revenue structure and per-user monetization ceilings sit below one dollar per month, so the business model was never viable<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-76">[3-76]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. The viable paths identified were three different businesses: consumer subscription companionship, open source plus B2B PaaS, and enterprise-internal agent marketplaces. The acquisition implication is direct — **a low barrier to creation floods supply, and a platform without curation capability equals zero distribution**; agents as an entry point are more likely to succeed as "enterprise utility agents with in-ecosystem distribution" than as a UGC marketplace revenue-share model.

**The second falsification is that the channel matured as a franchise scheme before it matured as a service.** GEO vendors have turned the business into recruiting city agents: heavy franchise advertising on Douyin, Xiaohongshu, and WeChat Moments, pulling the existing client bases of tax advisory, advertising, and business-services firms into GEO distribution. The pitch is usually "zero franchise fee," but initial inventory purchases, compute prepayments, system fees, deposits, and back-end packages ensure that something collects the money; one vendor quotes city-exclusive agent status at 50,000 yuan of initial inventory, with 50%–70% commissions and a self-claimed payback in two to three months. The real problem is a supply-demand mismatch: GEO genuinely suits businesses with an established brand, users who really do ask AI questions, and the capacity to keep producing credible content over time (national consumer brands, high-ticket B2B, large manufacturers, brands dependent on tendering or channel recruitment), whereas regional agents most easily reach restaurants, renovation firms, training schools, and small workshops — **even if a vendor's ten designed questions show 100% mention rates, that does not mean any meaningful number of consumers actually asked them**<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-77">[3-77]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

Together, the two falsifications suggest a crude way to judge channel maturity: **look at whether the money in a channel flows mainly toward "creating results for customers" or toward "selling systems to channel partners."** When the latter dominates, the channel is still in its bubble phase, and the noise outweighs the signal.

## 3.15 The Methodology of AI Visibility: A Single Snapshot Will Lie

The readings in Section 3.13 (ChatGPT's citations of Reddit plunging from about 60% to about 10% within weeks) make one thing clear: **AI visibility is not a quantity that can be "measured once."** Through the second half of 2026, several mutually corroborating studies emerged on how to measure it so the result doesn't expire.

**First, "when it expires" has been quantified.** AirOps, with Kevin Indig, in The 2026 State of AI Search uses millions of data points to produce stability figures: only 30% of brands stay visible in two adjacent answers, and only 20% appear five times in a row; pages not updated quarterly are 3x more likely to lose AI citations, while pages using sequential headings and rich schema are 2.8x more likely to be cited; about 48% of citations come from community platforms such as Reddit and YouTube, and 85% of brand mentions come from third-party pages rather than a brand's own domain<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-82">[3-82]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. The same report notes that about 60% of Google AI Overview citations come from URLs that never entered the natural-search top 20 — **AI citation is a second channel that can bypass traditional ranking.**

**Second, "how many times to measure" now has an academic answer.** Researchers at the University of St. Gallen tracked four industry events across a 45–46 day window from January to March 2026 and found that the Jaccard similarity of cited sources on adjacent days was only 0.34–0.42 — roughly 65% of cited sources turn over on an average day — with even lower RBO scores (0.21–0.26). The authors note that in the traditional search era vendors offered transparent, stable snapshots through native tools such as Search Console, whereas LLM vendors currently provide no equivalent monitoring tool, creating an "observability gap": measuring GEO performance requires repeated sampling rather than a single snapshot<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-83">[3-83]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. This turns sampling frequency and statistical significance from a methodology debate into a procurement standard.

**Third, a map of citation sources.** In its Q1 2026 Citation Source Audit, which combines nine independent studies, PR firm 5W finds that Wikipedia (13.15%) and Reddit (11.97%) together account for more than 25% of U.S. ChatGPT citations; The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times, Bloomberg, and the FT all fall outside the top 20, Forbes is the only U.S. business outlet to make the list (No. 18, 1.38%), YouTube's correlation with AI visibility is 0.737 — the single strongest predictor in 2025–2026 research — and LinkedIn rose within three months from No. 11 to No. 5 in ChatGPT, at a 14.3% citation rate<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-84">[3-84]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Its conclusion: "AI engines don't rank authorities; they assemble answers" — traditional PR hierarchies and AI reality have come apart.

**And that map is grown market by market.** Profound's quarterly index (based on more than 190 million real user conversations across 50-plus industries) finds that only seven brands lead consistently across markets, with Toyota and McDonald's ranking first in every market where data is available, while other categories are largely led by local brands; in markets where a "new local leader" has emerged, 46%–59% of citations flow to domestic domains, versus 12–17 percentage points lower where no local leader exists — **translated content is not enough to raise AI visibility across borders**<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-85">[3-85]</a></sup>[official claims].

**The traffic ledger already looks grim.** Data cited by Onely shows that since mid-2024 organic click-through on queries with an AI Overview has fallen 61% (from 1.76% to 0.61%); when an AI summary appears, only 8% of users click a traditional result versus 15% when it does not (Pew Research, a 47% drop); about 60% of searches end with zero clicks, and Gartner predicts traditional search-engine search volume will fall 25% by 2026<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-86">[3-86]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Put together, the operational takeaway is blunt: **before putting "AI visibility" into the budget, accept that it is a probability distribution rather than a ranking — it requires repeated sampling, market-by-market building, and simultaneous accounting for the loss of traditional search assets.**

## 3.16 Entry Points Re-Valued: Super-Apps Turn Services into Callable Atomic Capabilities

Section 3.10 described the Chinese search box turning into a dialogue box. This round's change is more foundational: WeChat and Alipay are rebuilding their "mini-programs and services" into atomic capabilities that AI can call directly, moving a brand's service from "waiting for someone to open it" to "being dispatched by AI."

**Two technical paths.** Reporters from 21st Century Business Herald tested Alipay's "Abao" and interviewed WeChat mini-program developers, describing two ways to break the wall. Alipay takes a dual-track approach — pushing mini-programs to encapsulate their functions as standardized AI skills shown as cards, while, with user authorization, having the AI read and operate mini-programs not yet converted. WeChat emphasizes voluntary developer authorization, adding an "AI capability" entry to its management console split into an "automatic mode" (developers authorize WeChat's AI to read pages and code) and a "development mode" (developers encapsulate functions and presentation themselves, subject to review before AI can call them); its developer documentation treats "atomic interfaces, atomic components, and Skills" as the three core units and sets call priority and anti-hallucination and privacy constraints (key fields must come from genuine upstream API returns, and sensitive information is masked)<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-87">[3-87]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. On scale, QuestMobile shows that in March 2026 WeChat, Alipay, and Douyin mini-programs had 973 million, 644 million, and 273 million monthly active users respectively, with WeChat in absolute dominance. The article also pours cold water: an insider at a leading lifestyle app says few people actually use it in lifestyle scenarios, and about 70% of the clients served by one developer abandon operating a mini-program after a few months, fearing that "if AI favors the big platforms, how will small and mid-sized merchants ever get a turn."

**It is a near-simultaneous infrastructure push.** Leiphone laid out a timeline of the two giants' moves from May to July 2026: Alipay launched a full-stack AI payments product in May, released the AI version of Alipay, "Abao," in June, and opened an AI platform on July 7 to merchants, institutions, service providers, smart terminals, and large-model platforms; in almost the same window, WeChat released AI-ecosystem access guidelines, introduced an AI-dedicated card, and began beta-testing its native assistant "Xiaowei." The article draws an analogy to QR-code payments a decade earlier — the two companies "rarely make such close moves around the same underlying commercial interface in the same window of time." On payment protocols, Alipay released version 2.0 of its ACT protocol (Agentic Commerce Trust Protocol) in April, co-built by IIFAA with Alipay, Zhipu, and 20-plus other vendors, while WeChat gave AI "a card that is controllable, rechargeable, and spendable"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-88">[3-88]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. For merchants, this may be a reshaping of "where business happens."

**Merchants are already queuing to get connected.** At its first AI ecosystem conference on August 17, Alipay disclosed that its super-service agent "Abao" has completed AI conversion of more than ten thousand services, supports five major phone brands and 16 major automakers across terminals, and launched a full-stack agentic-commerce foundation and a multi-agent cross-terminal interconnection protocol framework, the AHA protocol, co-built with Qwen, Huawei, OPPO, BYD, Geely and 20-plus other companies; Ant Group CEO Han Xinyi (Chinese: 韩歆毅) said "agentic commerce will break out in the next 6–12 months," and the president of Alipay's business group said that since the AI development platform launched in July, merchant requests have been so brisk that "some development demand is already scheduled into next year"<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-89">[3-89]</a></sup>[official claims]. Incentives for merchants include 100 million free tokens per person, payment-fee reductions, and token subsidies on real transactions, plus free access to 57 of Alipay's proprietary atomic operating capabilities — **one integration, with which "Abao" distributes the service to phones, car systems, AI glasses, and other terminals.**

**A third-party startup is betting on "attributable AI channels."** Science and Technology Daily reports on DeepLumen, a Chinese agentic-commerce startup whose product Agentic Page has been installed by more than 2,000 merchants across apparel, home, luggage, department stores, 3C digital, pets, and other categories, and which claims its merchants continuously generate "attributable orders from the AI channel," in the fastest cases closing a sale on day one. Its approach is to stop worrying about whether AI crawls or mentions the brand and instead reorganize product and brand information into structured content that AI can more easily understand and call, continuously syncing price and inventory; its deliverable is not "being understood by AI" but tracking the performance of orders that AI referrals bring into the merchant's site<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-90">[3-90]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**"The entry point is distribution" is the shared judgment this round.** Securities Times calls 2026 the "year one of general AI entry points," starting with Alibaba's new Qwen app on January 15 fully integrating Taobao, Alipay, Fliggy, and Amap: Qwen competes for the "AI shopping agent entry point," Doubao for the "video-and-entertainment agent entry point," and Yuanbao explores a "social agent entry point"; the analyst Tian Feng (Chinese: 田丰) judges that both traffic and tokens depend on "entry agents" dispatching orders, and that losing the next-generation entry point could let a giant decline quickly<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-91">[3-91]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Scale data supports the direction: National Business Daily with QuestMobile's Q1 2026 AI App Value Ranking shows that as of March 2026 China's AI apps reached 446 million users overall, with the top five apps' combined monthly actives rising from 355 million to 722 million within half a year; Doubao led in Q1 with 345 million monthly actives (+44.2% quarter-on-quarter), Qwen rose to second with 166 million (+969.1%), and DeepSeek fell back to 127 million, declining for a fourth straight quarter<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-92">[3-92]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**Yet the economics of the same entry points are deteriorating.** 36Kr's analysis of the content-commerce landscape after the new rules offers the other side: after China's first dedicated live-commerce rule, the Administrative Measures for the Supervision of Live-Streaming E-Commerce, took effect in February 2026, AIGC content such as digital-human streamers must be labeled and influencer product recommendations are classified as commercial advertising; Douyin's 2025 GMV was about 4.4 trillion yuan (2.75 times Kuaishou's), but growth has slowed to about 15–20%; **the average CPM of Juliang Qianchuan rose from 80 yuan in 2023 to 120 yuan in 2026, a 50% increase**<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-93">[3-93]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. As entry points increasingly resemble "AI dispatching," the unit cost of paid traffic rises in step — the two lines together are the real situation of customer acquisition in 2026.

## 3.17 The Third Reading on Zero-Click: Clicks Are Re-Allocated, Not Disappearing

Section 3.15 was about how a single snapshot lies. By mid-2026 the quantity itself had been re-measured three more times, and all three readings point the same way.

**The first reading is the total.** Using Similarweb's desktop and mobile panels for U.S. Google searches from January to April 2026, SparkToro calculates that 68.01% of queries end with zero clicks, versus 60.45% in the same period of 2024 — a rise of 7.5 percentage points in two years (+12.5%), the fastest acceleration in a decade (about 45% in 2016 and 49% in 2019). Mechanically, AI Overviews now appear on more than 20% of searches and cut click-through by nearly 60% where they do; the share of searches producing one click or more fell 9.51 percentage points over two years (−22.9%), while the share of repeat searches rose 7.2 percentage points. One counterintuitive detail: only 0.34% of searches entered AI Mode in January–April 2026, so AI Overviews — not AI Mode — are behind this round of zero-click growth; the author reminds readers that search demand has not vanished, clicks have been re-allocated<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-94">[3-94]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**The second reading is a longitudinal site-side comparison.** Define Media Group analysed Search Console data across its 64 sites: organic search clicks are down 42% since AI Overviews expanded. From Q1 2023 to Q1 2024 the sites averaged about 1.7 billion clicks per quarter; the moment AIO launched that fell 16% and never recovered, and the decline accelerated after AIO expanded further in May 2025. Crucially the losses are uneven — concentrated in informational and evergreen content, while AIO fires less often on news queries, leaving news and Discover relatively open: **channel decay is regressive rather than uniform, and how much you lose depends on your content type**<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-95">[3-95]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**The third reading is a page-level controlled comparison.** Seer Interactive tracked 2.43 billion impressions, 53 brands, and 5.47 million queries and finds organic CTR on AIO queries falling from 1.76% to 0.61% (−65%), rebounding to 2.4% in February 2026, with the click gap between AIO and non-AIO queries still around 37%; over the same period AIO covered roughly 48–50% of U.S. Google queries, versus just 6.49% in January 2025<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-96">[3-96]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. The rebound is worth noting: **this is the first public data showing AIO click-through recovering, which suggests readers' attention to AI summaries has not yet settled.**

**On the supply side, the response is to cut off.** Nieman Lab records a 2026 turning point: Semrush data shows some publications losing more than 40% of traffic between June 2025 and June 2026 (The Guardian and the BBC grew instead); Cloudflare says bots now account for more than half of all web traffic and, from September 15, blocks "multi-purpose crawlers" (those used both for search indexing and AI training) by default unless site owners opt in. Some publishers are now discussing cutting off Google entirely: the CEO of USA Today says the company is "100% considering" it, and Reddit is re-evaluating its roughly $60 million annual licensing deal because of traffic declines<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-97">[3-97]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. Three readings plus one reaction add up to the real constraint on 2026 acquisition budgets: **search demand remains, but the segment from search to site is narrowing — and for the first time that narrowing is quantified at roughly four percentage points a year.**

## 3.18 The Second Layer of Visibility: Indexing Switches, Trust Audits, and Purchases That Happen Outside the Chat

The previous section followed where traffic leaks out. This one asks a different question: **once the answer itself becomes the entry point, who decides whether you get in, and on what grounds you get mentioned.**

**The switch has been handed to site owners first.** On August 31, 2026, Google rolled out "Search generative AI control" to all sites worldwide, with three settings: include (the default — content may appear in AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Discover's generative features as links that help ground the answer), exclude (links and content are invisible and unlinkable in those features and cannot be used as input to generative answers, and the site receives no exposure), and inherit the parent property's setting. Google states explicitly that this is not a ranking or indexing signal, does not affect other parts of Search, and does not override participation choices in Merchant Center or Google Ads; limiting training requires Google-Extended and leaving Search entirely requires noindex. Changes typically take effect within one to two days<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-98">[3-98]</a></sup>[official]. **This is a classic case of handing risk-pricing power back to the supply side**: whether AI may use your content becomes a contract question rather than a default fact.

**The split on the crawler side has been laid out in data.** SearchScore audited the robots.txt policies of 347,988 unique domains (a 90-day window ending August 18, 2026): the training crawler GPTBot is blocked by 13.5% of sites, while the search crawler OAI-SearchBot is blocked by only 7.83%; 19,998 domains block GPTBot while explicitly allowing OAI-SearchBot, against only 270 configured the other way — a directional ratio of about 74:1<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-99">[3-99]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. OpenAI's own crawler documentation and its publishers FAQ draw the line even more clearly: blocking OAI-SearchBot keeps a site out of ChatGPT search answers, blocking GPTBot signals that content is not for training, and OAI-AdsBot visits only pages submitted as ads and does not use what it collects for training<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-100">[3-100]</a></sup>; the publishers FAQ separates the two intentions explicitly — do not block OAI-SearchBot if you want your content in ChatGPT summaries and snippets, and disallow GPTBot if you do not want it used for potential training<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-101">[3-101]</a></sup>[official]. Cloudflare's filing to the UK's Competition and Markets Authority aims at the structure itself, arguing that only separating AI crawling from search crawling can produce a fair internet; the CMA designated Google as having "strategic market status" in October 2025 on the strength of its roughly 90% share of UK search, and that designation covers AI Overviews and AI Mode, giving the regulator power to impose legally binding requirements on AI crawling terms<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-102">[3-102]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. **Both "may you crawl" and "will you be allowed to crawl" are now negotiable commercial terms, and gaps in bargaining power translate directly into gaps in visibility.**

**The other thread is on what grounds you get mentioned.** The Credibility Paradox, published by BCW with the AI marketing platform Profound, tested thousands of reputation-related answers across seven major AI question-answering platforms, assessed 85 companies, and produced more than 55,000 credibility predictions: **AI favours evidence over positioning** — objective factual statements about innovation, products, and workplace culture consistently outperform subjective attributes such as leadership, governance, and corporate citizenship; "workplace" is an underrated lever because hiring-platform reviews, labour reports, and earned media can be independently verified, while "leadership" ranks last across industries; and business decision-makers trust AI answers about 10% more than the general public does. The flagship conclusion: visibility is only a necessary condition — appearing in a model is not the same as persuading the audience<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-103">[3-103]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. The local-business reading is just as steep: BrightLocal surveyed 1,002 U.S. consumers and found the share using AI to find local business recommendations jumping from 6% in 2025 to 45%, making AI the third-largest local recommendation tool ahead of Yelp and TripAdvisor (ChatGPT 31%, Google AI Mode 23%), while reliance on Google for reviews fell from 83% to 71%<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-104">[3-104]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**But don't reverse the conclusion.** Attentive surveyed more than 3,000 U.S. consumers and cites Adobe data showing AI-sourced traffic to retail sites up 62% year over year in July 2026, with those visitors behaving better: 59% longer dwell time, 22% more pages per visit, 60% higher conversion, and 53% more revenue per visit. The structural finding, however, points the other way: **only 10% of buyers complete their purchase inside the AI, and 60% of AI-assisted shoppers would rather buy on the site or app where the product lives**; 93% still seek corroboration outside AI before deciding<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-105">[3-105]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. That explains the pattern — AI is not a new checkout, it is a new **first mirror**: buyers use it as a filter, but the last step comes back to a place you control. For acquisition teams this means AI visibility KPIs should not stop at "how often were we mentioned," but extend to "after the mention, did people come back to our turf" and "what evidence does the model use to judge us."

## 3.19 The Second Check-Up for AI SDRs: The Gap Is Narrowing, and It Isn't the Win Rate

Chapter 3 opened with a self-built AI SDR test (400 hours to move reply rate from 1% to 2%). This round adds a larger, like-for-like comparison.

**The like-for-like comparison says "close but still behind."** Digital Applied analysed 100,000 paired cold emails — 50,000 AI-generated and 50,000 human-written, matched on persona, ICP firmographics, sequence stage, sending-domain age, and domain authority, delivered over a six-month window: AI reply rate 4.1% versus 5.2% for humans; "effective reply rate" (excluding out-of-office, explicit rejections, and unsubscribes) 1.4% versus 2.1%; meeting rate 0.7% versus 1.1%; and identical bounce rates (6% for both), indicating that bounces are a list-quality problem rather than a writing problem. The trend is what deserves recording: in the same methodology's 2024 dataset, AI reply rate was 2.8%, so over 18 months the human–AI gap narrowed from 2.0 to 1.1 percentage points (a reduction of about 45%)<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-106">[3-106]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**Survival in production looks worse than reply rate.** Drawing on what it describes as 18 months of AI SDR deployments for 23 clients, OneAway offers benchmarks: in Q2 2026, 41% of enterprise B2B teams were running at least one AI SDR in production; outbound volume per rep rose from 1,150 to 7,360 touches a month (6.4x), but positive reply rate fell from 2.1% for purely human outreach to 1.3% (−38%); and 40–60% of pilots were paused or shut down within 90 days, mostly for poor results, deliverability problems, or compliance. The checkable external view: Bain Capital Ventures (April 2026) says fully autonomous AI SDRs have not yet replaced human sales teams at scale. The optimal configuration the piece identifies is not "more autonomy" but a **hybrid (one human plus four AI agents)** — a 22% opportunity conversion rate, above 18% for all-human and 12% for fully autonomous AI, and the fastest-growing organizational form<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-107">[3-107]</a></sup>[third-party verified].

**The hard thresholds on the receiving side are rising too.** Bulk outbound in 2026 is no longer a free-for-all: Gmail, Yahoo, Microsoft, and Apple all require SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and one-click unsubscribe, with differing complaint-rate ceilings; one guide sets out to correct a widely repeated error — Gmail's spam complaint-rate threshold is 0.08%, not the 0.3% most articles cite<sup><a href="12-appendix-a-sources.md#3-108">[3-108]</a></sup>[third-party verified]. A threshold off by an order of magnitude means a sending cadence designed to the wrong number may already be over the line. Read together, the sensible 2026 positioning for AI SDRs is this: **they have solved outbound's capacity problem, left the quality problem to process design, and moved the risk from "writing badly" to "failing to deliver."**

## 3.20 Summary

Pulling Chapter 3 together, the picture of AI-powered prospecting is:

- **AI SDRs and agentic sales** scale outbound capacity a hundredfold, but independent data shows human sellers still hold a clear quality edge — the hybrid model is the mainstream;
- **Content acquisition and GEO** turn "writing content" into "manufacturing traffic assets," moving the battlefield from search engines to AI search;
- **ABM × intent data** shift B2B acquisition from "casting a wide net" to "precision-guided strikes" — 70% of a purchase is mostly complete before the vendor is ever contacted;
- **Private-domain AI** addresses the Chinese market's stock-operations problem of "added, then silent";
- **AI-powered paid media** turns budget allocation into an algorithmic problem;
- **The backlash warning**: AI outbound is killing cold email — balancing the efficiency dividend against channel trust is the first lesson of AI-driven acquisition.
